Use for individuals paid to work events with Company, such as coordinators, assistants and setup or teardown crew. (Businesses that sell directly to clients sign the Preferred Vendor & Referral Agreement instead.)

1. Independent contractor.

Contractor is an independent contractor, not an employee, partner, agent or joint venturer of Company. Contractor is not eligible for employee benefits, workers' compensation, unemployment insurance, overtime pay or tax withholding through Company. Contractor is responsible for all of their own federal and state taxes, including self-employment tax. Contractor may not sign contracts or make promises for Company or its clients.

2. Bookings.

Company will offer work through a booking confirmation (the form attached, or a text or email with the same details). Contractor may accept or decline any booking. Once Contractor accepts, the booking becomes part of this agreement. Company does not guarantee any amount of work, and Contractor is free to work for others, including other planners, vendors and venues.

3. How the work is done.

Company sets the results needed for each event: the timeline, the client's requirements and the venue rules. Contractor uses their own skill and judgment to decide how to deliver those results. Contractor provides their own transportation, phone and the basic tools their role normally requires. Contractor may send a qualified substitute only with Company's approval first. Contractor pays the substitute. Before working any event, the substitute must sign Company's Confidentiality Agreement and Non-Solicitation Agreement.

4. Pay.

Company will pay the rate above within 14 days after each event and after receiving Contractor's invoice or confirmation of hours. Hourly work runs from the call time in the booking until Company's on-site lead confirms Contractor is done. Hours beyond the booking must be approved by Company in advance (a text is fine).

Expenses are not reimbursed unless Company approves them in writing in advance and Contractor provides receipts.

Tips a client gives directly to Contractor belong to Contractor. Tips a client gives to Company for the whole team will be split evenly among everyone who worked that event.

Tax forms. Contractor will give Company a completed IRS Form W-9 before the first payment; no payment is made until it is received. Company will issue Form 1099-NEC if payments for the year reach the IRS reporting threshold.

5. Standards.

At each event, Contractor will:

  • arrive on time, follow the event timeline and dress as stated above;
  • follow venue rules and treat clients, guests and vendors professionally;
  • not drink alcohol, use drugs, or smoke or vape in guest areas while working, and not bring personal guests;
  • tell Company right away about any injury, accident, damage, safety issue or guest complaint.

Contractor confirms they are at least 18 years old and legally allowed to work in the United States.

6. Cancellations and no-shows.

If Contractor must cancel a confirmed booking, they will give at least the notice shown above, except in a genuine emergency, and will help find a replacement if they can. A no-show without notice, or repeated late cancellations, lets Company end this agreement immediately. Company may cancel or shorten any booking at any time and will try to give as much notice as it can. Contractor is paid only for work actually performed. No pay is owed for a cancelled booking or for hours not worked. If Contractor has already arrived and started work when an event is cut short, Company pays for the hours worked (or, for a per-event rate, the full rate).

7. Confidentiality.

Company will give Contractor access to confidential information, including client names and contact details, budgets, contracts, guest information, pricing, vendor terms and Company's planning methods and templates. Contractor will use it only to work Company events, will not share it, and will return or delete it when this agreement ends. This obligation continues after the agreement ends.

8. Client non-solicitation.

In exchange for the confidential information and client relationships Company provides, for 12 months after Contractor's last event with Company, Contractor will not, directly or through anyone else, offer or provide event planning or coordination services to any client Contractor met or learned about through Company, or encourage any client to stop or reduce working with Company. If such a client contacts Contractor directly, Contractor will refer them to Company. This does not stop Contractor from working for other planners, vendors or venues, or from serving people Contractor knew before working with Company. If a court finds this section too broad, it may narrow it to what is enforceable under Texas law.

9. Photos, social media and work product.

Contractor will not post photos of a client's event, or tag or name the client, without Company's approval. Contractor allows Company to use photos of Contractor working at events in Company's marketing. Timelines, layouts, checklists and other materials Contractor creates for Company events belong to Company.

10. Property.

Contractor will take reasonable care of Company and client property and will pay for loss or damage caused by Contractor's negligence or misconduct. Any deduction from Contractor's pay for this requires Contractor's written agreement.

11. Insurance and injuries.

Company does not carry workers' compensation or health insurance for Contractor. Contractor is responsible for their own health and injury coverage and for auto insurance required by Texas law when driving to and from events.

12. Indemnity.

CONTRACTOR WILL DEFEND, INDEMNIFY AND HOLD HARMLESS COMPANY AND ITS OWNER FROM ALL CLAIMS, DAMAGES AND COSTS, INCLUDING REASONABLE ATTORNEY'S FEES, TO THE EXTENT CAUSED BY CONTRACTOR'S NEGLIGENCE, MISCONDUCT OR BREACH OF THIS AGREEMENT.

13. Ending the agreement.

Either party may end this agreement at any time by written notice. Ending the agreement cancels any bookings not yet worked, and Company owes pay only for work already performed, as in Section 6. Sections 4 (for pay owed for work performed), 7 through 12, and 14 continue after the agreement ends.

14. Texas law and disputes.

This agreement is governed by Texas law, with venue in Tarrant County, Texas. Company may ask a court to stop a breach of Sections 7 through 9 right away, in addition to any other remedy.

15. General.

Written notice includes email or text to the contacts in this agreement (Company: makeithappenevents.dfw@gmail.com, 817-458-8699). This agreement and each accepted booking confirmation are the complete agreement. Changes must be in writing and approved by both parties. If any part is found unenforceable, it will be limited to the extent needed and the rest still applies. Contractor may not transfer this agreement except as allowed in Section 3. Electronic signatures and signed copies count as originals.
